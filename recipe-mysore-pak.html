Mysore Pak

Also known as Mysuru Pak in Karnataka

M
South India Autumn · Diwali Snack Vegetarian Festive
Sign in to save this recipe.

Ingredients

For the sugar syrup

  • 1 cup sugar (200 g)
  • ½ cup water

For the besan mixture

  • 1 cup besan (gram flour / chickpea flour), sieved
  • 1 cup ghee, plus ¼ cup neutral oil
  • A pinch of cardamom powder (optional)

Method

  1. Grease a tray or plate generously with ghee and keep it ready — the mixture sets fast.
  2. In a heavy pan, dissolve the sugar in the water and boil to one-string consistency: a drop pressed between thumb and finger pulls a single thread.
  3. Lower the flame and add the sieved besan a spoonful at a time, stirring constantly so no lumps form.
  4. Heat the ghee and oil together until very hot. Pour a ladleful into the besan mixture and stir; it will froth and bubble. Keep stirring until the fat is absorbed, then repeat 4–5 times.
  5. When the mixture turns pale gold, smells nutty, and leaves the sides of the pan, pour it into the greased tray immediately and level gently.
  6. After 5 minutes, cut into squares while still warm. Cool fully (about 30 minutes) before separating. Store airtight.
Kid-friendly version

Mysore pak is already vegetarian and nut-free. For young cooks: let an adult handle the hot sugar syrup and the pouring — kids can grease the tray, sieve the besan, and sprinkle the cardamom. Cut into small bite-size squares; the rich ghee means a little goes a long way.

Story behind this dish

In 1935, Kakasura Madappa, the chief cook of the Mysore Palace, was asked by King Krishna Raja Wadiyar IV for a brand-new sweet. With gram flour, ghee, and sugar at hand, he improvised — and the melt-in-the-mouth fudge that emerged was named “Mysuru paka,” a sweet from Mysore. It remains the Diwali sweet of Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, made in vast trays for gifting and temple prasad.

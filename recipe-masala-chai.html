Masala Chai

Also known as Cutting Chai in Mumbai

C
India Winter Drink Vegetarian
Sign in to save this recipe.

Ingredients

For the spice mix

  • 4 green cardamom pods (chhoti elaichi), crushed
  • 1-inch cinnamon stick (dalchini)
  • 4 cloves (laung)
  • 1-inch fresh ginger (adrak), sliced

For the chai (2 cups)

  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tsp Assam black tea leaves
  • 1 cup milk
  • Sugar to taste

Method

  1. Crush the cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and ginger coarsely with a mortar and pestle.
  2. Bring the water to a rolling boil, add the crushed spices, and simmer 2–3 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Add the tea leaves and boil 1 minute more for strength.
  4. Pour in the milk and sugar; bring back to a boil, then simmer 2 minutes until the color turns a rich caramel brown.
  5. Strain into cups and serve piping hot.
Kid-friendly version

Caffeine-free “chai” for kids: swap the black tea for rooibos leaves (same dark color, no caffeine), keep the spices and milk, and go light on the sugar. Always let an adult handle the boiling and pouring.

Story behind this dish

Tea arrived in India on British plantations, but Indians made the bitter colonial brew their own — simmered with milk, sugar, and the spice box: ginger (adrak), cardamom (elaichi), cloves. The half-glass “cutting chai” of Mumbai's trains and street corners turned it into the country's everyday ritual.

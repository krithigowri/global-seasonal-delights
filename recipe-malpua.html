Malpua

Batter

  • 1 cup refined flour (maida)
  • 2 tbsp semolina (suji)
  • 1 cup milk
  • 2 tbsp jaggery or sugar (for the batter)
  • 1/4 tsp green cardamom powder
  • 1/4 tsp fennel seeds (optional)
  • A pinch of salt
  • Ghee, for shallow frying

Syrup

  • 3/4 cup sugar or grated jaggery
  • 1/3 cup water
  • 1/4 tsp cardamom powder
  • A few saffron strands (optional)

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, semolina, milk, jaggery or sugar, cardamom, fennel and salt into a smooth batter of pouring consistency, like a thick dosa batter. Rest 20-30 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, simmer the sugar or jaggery with water, cardamom and saffron until the sweetener dissolves fully (a light syrup — no thread consistency needed for jaggery). Keep warm.
  3. Heat ghee in a wide, shallow pan over medium flame. Ladle in small rounds of batter without spreading — they set like mini pancakes. Fry until golden and crisp at the edges, flipping once.
  4. Lift out, drain briefly, and dip each malpua in the warm syrup for a few seconds. Serve hot, stacked, as prasad.

Malpua for children

Malpua is a natural children's sweet — the same soft, syrup-soaked pancakes the family carries to the ghats are handed to children as prasad. Families often serve children the freshly fried malpua plain, without the final syrup dip, which is how many kids prefer it.

“Kids love it” means flavors young taste buds enjoy — not safety for every child. Adults must supervise knives, heat and appliances — written for adults to prepare with children.

Story behind this dish

Malpua's place in Chhath is as the celebratory pancake of the ghats: TV9 English notes it holds a special place in Chhath Puja celebrations as a symbol of abundance and devotion — fried in ghee and soaked lightly in sugar or jaggery syrup, sometimes flavoured with fennel seeds and coconut, and carried in the daura (offering basket) to the riverbank for Sandhya and Usha Arghya. Restaurant India describes its signature contrast: a crispy exterior with a soft interior that must be eaten fresh.
